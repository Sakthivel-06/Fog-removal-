A B S T R A C T
In this work, a single image dehazing method that improves the haze removal capacity of the Joint Contrast
Enhancement and Exposure Fusion (CEEF) method with Smoothing-Sharpening Image Filter (SSIF) is presented.
In this method, the hazy image is first sharpened with SSIF to obtain a sharper image. In this way, the difference
between haze and objects is amplified. Then, the AHE procedure in CEEF is replaced by CLAHE to obtain
an enhanced CEEF. The enhanced CEEF is applied to the filtering result to obtain the final dehazed image.
Observations demonstrate that the proposed method obtains enhanced results while reducing the amount of
haze. The visual and quantitative comparisons between the proposed method and state-of-the-art dehazing
methods show that the proposed method has better dehazing performance and has a 50% improvement in
terms of the FADE metric compared to the closest result.
1. Introduction
Imaging systems tend to construct low contrast images, especially
in bad weather conditions like fog and haze. The low visibility of these
images is dependent on the scattering and absorption of the light by
the particles within the atmosphere. Since most automated systems and
advanced image processing algorithms need higher contrast images,
dehazing is a fundamental problem for these applications.
Since the amount of haze depends on the distance between the scene
and the imaging device, the depth information of these images is not
present. Therefore, image dehazing is a challenging problem because of
the unknown depth information. The traditional enhancement methods,
like histogram-based methods, cannot restore the depth information of
the images [1–3].
In order to increase the performance of dehazing algorithms, multi-
ple images of the same scene taken under different weather conditions
are used [4–6]. These algorithms are effective for haze removal; how-
ever, the need for multiple scenes for input is generally not possible.
For this reason, many single image dehazing algorithms have been
developed [7–10]. These methods generally use a prior or assumption
along with the common hazy image model. The standard model consists
of two unknown parameters, namely transmission and airlight. The
main objective of single image dehazing methods is to estimate the
transmission map. In order to achieve this, several prior assumptions
have been made. For instance, He et al. [ 7] proposed using the Dark
Channel Prior (DCP) for dehazing. The prior calculates the darkest pixel
within a patch based on the observation that at least one pixel has a low

intensity for at least one color channel. Although an effective method, it
distorts the color, especially in the sky region, and the time consump-
tion is too high due to the soft matting process to reduce the effects
caused by patches. Zhu et al. [ 9] have proposed a color attenuation
prior for image dehazing with a supervised learning process. In this
prior, the difference between the saturation and brightness values of
the hazy image based linear model is constructed to estimate the haze
amount within the image. The method does not perform well, especially
for in-homogeneous haze. Recently, a multi-scale products prior based
method (MP) has been proposed [ 10]. This method decomposes the
input hazy image by an undecimated Laplacian decomposition to obtain
its detail and approximation subbands. Then, the multi-scale product
of the approximation subband is used to calculate the prior. This prior
can remove the haze and does not distort the color in the sky region.
However, the method cannot remove the haze, especially in the mid
regions. The prior-based algorithms generally suffer from artifacts and
distortions.
Due to the improvements in learning algorithms, image dehazing
methods based on learning have attracted researchers in recent years.
Learning-based methods can be divided into two categories model-
based, and model-free methods [11]. Model-based methods directly try
to estimate the transmission maps with the use of convolutional neural
networks. Cai et al. [12] have proposed a trainable system (DehazeNet)
to estimate the transmission with the help of convolutional neural
networks. Ren et al. [ 13] proposed a multi-scale deep neural network.

The deep network model is trained to learn the mapping between the
transmission map and the hazy image in this method. The model-based
algorithms generally suffer from artifacts and distortions similar to the
prior-based methods. On the other hand, model-free methods do not
use the classical hazy image model. Instead, the methods dehaze the
image by using end-to-end convolutional neural networks. For instance,
GridDehazeNet is proposed in [14]. The method uses an end-to-end
convolutional network consisting of three parts: pre-processing, back-
bone, and post-processing. The model-free learning algorithms perform
better than the model-based learning algorithms. However, they still
present artifacts and distortions in the dehazed images [15].
In addition to the methods given above, there are dehazing methods
based on image enhancement techniques, like Retinex [16] and fu-
sion [17–19]. In [16], it is assumed that there is a relationship between
dehazing and Retinex. The dehazing results sometimes have unnatural
colors due to the fact that Retinex ignores the atmospheric light [11].
In [17], a Laplacian pyramid-based multi-scale fusion strategy is pro-
posed for dehazing. In [18], a guided filter-driven weighted combina-
tion of Gaussian-based DCP and an Adaptive Histogram Equalization is
used.
Recently, a Joint Contrast Enhancement and Exposure Fusion based
dehazing method (CEEF) has been proposed [11]. In this method,
firstly, contrast enhancement is performed. In this stage, Gamma Cor-
rection (GC) and Color Preserving Adaptive Histogram Equalization
(CP-AHE) is applied to improve the contrast of the image. After enhanc-
ing the contrast, a fast and adaptive structural patch decomposition is
applied to fuse the images obtained by GC and CP-AHE. Even though
providing better results than previous methods, the haze is not removed
enough, and the contrast improvement can be better.
Motivated by the results of CEEF [11], in this paper, a dehaz-
ing method that combines the Smoothing-Sharpening Image Filter
(SSIF) [20] and CEEF is proposed. The proposed method first sharpens
the images with SSIF. The sharpened image is expected to amplify the
difference between haze and objects within the image. Thus, the haze
removal performance is also expected to be amplified. After obtaining
the sharpened hazy image, an enhanced version of CEEF is applied
to the sharpened image to obtain the dehazed image. The proposed
enhanced CEEF replaces the Adaptive Histogram Equalization (AHE)
with Clipping Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) in
the contrast enhancement stage of the CEEF method. In this way, the
possible noise amplifications of AHE are expected to be reduced so as
to obtain a better enhancement performance. The main contributions
of the proposed dehazing algorithm can be concluded as follows:
• The input hazy image is sharpened by Sharpening Smoothing
Image Filter (SSIF) to amplify the difference between the scene
and haze so as to obtain a better haze removal performance.
• The Adaptive Histogram Equalization (AHE) provides noisy re-
sults in the enhanced results. Therefore, it is proposed to replace
the AHE of the CEEF method with Clipping Limited AHE (CLAHE)
to obtain a better-enhanced result.
A large dataset of hazy images has been used to examine the perfor-
mance of the proposed method. The resulting images and quantitative
comparisons show that the proposed dehazing algorithm produces
images with higher contrast and visibility.
The rest of the paper is as follows. An explanation of the SSIF and
CEEF is given in Section 2. The proposed dehazing method is given
in Section 3. Experimental setup, ablation studies, real-world image
dehazing, and comparisons for benchmark datasets are included in
Section 4. The paper is concluded in Section 5.

In the proposed method, parameter determination is also important.
There are four parameters of SSIF to be determined; namely, Patch Ra-
dius (𝑟), Scale (𝑠), Sharpening Gain𝜖, and sharpening-smoothing factor
(𝜅) [20]. Both real-world and synthetic hazy images have been dehazed
by the proposed method to understand the effects of the parameters.
After experimenting on different types of images, it is seen that the
𝑟, 𝑠, and 𝜖 parameters have a minor effect on dehazing results. On
the other hand, the 𝜅 parameter dramatically affects dehazed results.
Moreover, it is observed that the 𝜅 value differs for synthetic images
and real-world images.
In order to determine the 𝜅 parameter for synthetic images, ran-
domly chosen 40 images within the RESIDE [ 22] dataset are dehazed
with SSIF. Average Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) and
Peak-Signal-to-Noise-Ratio (PSNR) metrics were calculated for different
𝜅 values. The PSNR and SSIM values for differing 𝜅 values are shown
in Fig. 2. As seen in Fig. 2, the average SSIM and PSNR values indicate
that choosing𝜅 around 1.2 provides better results for synthetic images.
It is well known that real-world data has to be interpreted dif-
ferently than synthetic datasets. Therefore, to evaluate the 𝜅 value
for real-world images, the Fog Aware Density Evaluator (FADE) [ 23]
metric has been evaluated for differing 𝜅 values for randomly chosen
real-world images. The𝜅 value is determined as 2 for real-world images
according to average FADE values.
After determining the filtering parameters of the SSIF, the clipping
limit of CLAHE is also determined in a similar manner. The clipping
limit is chosen as 0.0017 for synthetic images and 0.02 for real-world
images.
4. Experimental results
4.1. Experimental setup
4.1.1. Dataset
In order to determine the performance of the proposed method, 32
known real-world scenes are used to evaluate the real-world dehazing
performance of the method. Moreover, synthetic datasets RESIDE [22],
O-HAZE [24], I-HAZE [25], D-HAZY [26], DENSE-HAZE [27], and NH-

500 Indoor hazy images and 492 Outdoor hazy images, while RESIDE
citeLi2019 HSTS contains ten hazy images. O-HAZE [ 24] dataset con-
tains 45 outdoor hazy images, whereas I-HAZE [ 25] dataset has 30
indoor hazy images. D-HAZY [ 26] Middlebury dataset has 23 hazy
images, and NYU dataset has 1449 hazy images. DENSE-HAZE [ 27]
dataset contains 55 high-density hazy images, while NH-HAZE [ 28]
contains 55 hazy images with non-homogeneous haze.

second-best performance in three datasets.
Even though the proposed method focuses on enhancement along
with dehazing, the PSNR and SSIM values obtained for benchmark
datasets indicate that the proposed method has a solid performance for
the synthetic datasets.
Considering real-world images, the proposed method has a superior
performance over compared state-of-the-art dehazing methods. For
synthetic hazy images, the overall performance of the proposed method
is close to or better than the compared state-of-art methods.
